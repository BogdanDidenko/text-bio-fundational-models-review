## RVQ-Alpha: Bridging Single-Cell Transcriptomics and Large Language Models via Discrete Tokenization and Verifiable Reinforcement Learning

Guangpeng Li 1,2,† , Yue You 1,† , Yunlin Fu 1 , Weige Zhou 1,3 Feng Tang 1 , Jiaming Kong 4, ∗ , Luyi Tian 1,3, ∗

1 Guangzhou National Laboratory, Guangzhou, China 2 Electronic Information Engineering Program, School of Computer Science and Engineering, South China University of Technology, Guangzhou, China 3 GMU-GIBH Joint School of Life Sciences, Guangzhou Medical University, Guangzhou, China 4 Rectified Flow Capital Limited, Hong Kong SAR, China

#### Abstract

Integrating continuous single-cell gene expression profiles with discrete-token large language models (LLMs) remains an open challenge: text-based methods are token-inefficient and discard quantitative precision, continuous embeddings preclude autoregressive generation, and single-codebook vector quantization cannot stratify biological scales.

We present RVQ-Alpha , an end-to-end framework that bridges this modality gap through three contributions:

(i) a Residual Vector Quantization (RVQ) tokenizer that compresses each cell into a fixed 10-token sequence via eight residual codebooks embedded directly in the LLM vocabulary, requiring 3.4 × fewer tokens than prior discrete methods and enabling bidirectional unification of cell interpretation and generation;

(ii) scCoT-Synth , a teacher-student engine that grounds newly added biological tokens through evidence-before-conclusion reasoning, using the language modeling objective as the cross-modal alignment signal without a separate projection network;

and (iii) a Fact-Aware RLVR system combining an ontology-grounded answer judge with saliency-weighted verification of biological claims against actual expression data, under dynamic gating that conditions hallucination suppression on task competence.

Built on Qwen3-4B and trained via continued pretraining, supervised fine-tuning, and reinforcement learning, RVQ-Alpha substantially improves out-of-distribution generalization and rare-cell recognition across eight held-out datasets; ablations confirm that evidence-first grounding reduces hallucination more than fivefold.

## 1 Introduction

Building computational models that understand and simulate cellular behavior, a vision termed the AI Virtual Cell [1], is a central goal of computational biology. Single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) [2] provides the high-resolution transcriptomic data required by such models, and large language models (LLMs) offer powerful reasoning and generation capabilities that could serve as their computational substrate [3, 4]. Realizing this vision demands a model that can both interpret existing cell states (e.g., cell type annotation, disease classification) and generate new cell states (e.g., perturbation simulation) within a unified architecture. We address a specific setting: compressing continuous gene expression profiles into discrete tokens that reside directly in the LLM vocabulary, enabling a single autoregressive model to achieve both the understanding and generation of cellular representations.

This problem is particularly challenging because it requires simultaneously preserving quantitative expression information, achieving token efficiency, and enabling autoregressive generation through the LLM vocabulary.

Single-cell foundation models have scaled rapidly (from scGPT [5] with 33M cells and Geneformer [6] with 30M cells, to CellFM [7] with 100M cells and 800M parameters, and scPRINT-2 [8] with 350M cells across 16 organisms); yet, systematic benchmarks show that no single model dominates across all downstream tasks (Tables 8 and 9) [9, 10, 11]. More fundamentally, these continuous-representation methods [5, 6, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16, 17, 18] learn cell embeddings through masked expression prediction; they produce representations that enable accurate classification but cannot be decoded back through an LLM vocabulary . This architectural limitation precludes autoregressive cell generation and prevents integration with natural language reasoning chains.

To overcome this vocabulary incompatibility, text-based approaches convert expression profiles into natural language that pre-trained LLMs can process directly, using either ranked gene lists [19, 20, 21, 22, 23] or structured textual descriptions enriched with biological knowledge [24, 25, 26, 27]. However, these approaches introduce a different fundamental trade-off: ranked gene lists discard quantitative expression precision and produce token-inefficient representations (40-80 tokens per cell for the top-20 genes), whereas structured descriptions scale poorly to population-level multi-cell reasoning [28]. Recent work has further enhanced text-based LLMs with reasoning capabilities (for example, CellReasoner [29] introduces chain-of-thought annotation from 380 exemplars, and Cello1 [30] applies reinforcement learning with batch-level rewards). Nevertheless, these approaches still operate on text-based gene representations and lack mechanisms to verify biological claims against actual expression data .

A concurrent line of work explores discrete tokenization to resolve this trade-off. CellTok [31] demonstrates that vector-quantized variational autoencoder (VQ-VAE) discrete tokens, integrated into the Qwen2.5 vocabulary via early fusion, enable previously unattainable population-level analyses. However, CellTok employs a single shared codebook that cannot allocate capacity across biological scales; furthermore, it does not incorporate structured reasoning and relies solely on supervised fine-tuning without reinforcement learning. The core technical challenge remains: how to achieve discrete tokenization that preserves multi-scale biological structure, combined with verifiable reasoning that grounds predictions in actual gene expression evidence .

We propose RVQ-Alpha , an end-to-end framework that resolves this challenge through three integrated innovations. Figure 1 illustrates the overall framework. Our core innovation is a Residual Vector Quantization (RVQ) autoencoder that compresses the expression profile of each cell into eight discrete tokens drawn from eight residual codebooks of 32 entries each (8 × 32 = 256 codes). This yields a variance-ordered decomposition that tracks cellular identity from broader lineage distinctions to finer functional states (a correspondence we validate empirically in §6), requiring approximately 60% fewer tokens than text-based representations. Because these tokens reside directly in the LLM vocabulary, the model can both interpret RVQ sequences for analytical tasks and generate new RVQ sequences for cell state simulation, thereby achieving bidirectional unification .

To further improve reasoning quality beyond what supervised learning alone can achieve, we construct a multi-component RLVR reward system [32] that evaluates answer accuracy via an LLM-as-judge approach [33] and assesses reasoning quality across five dimensions (independence, robustness, causality, granularity, and consistency). This expression-grounded verification extends the RLVR paradigm recently explored for biological reasoning [34] by providing fine-grained, cell-specific reward signals.

We evaluate RVQ-Alpha on a held-out pool of eight evaluation corpora curated from the ScBank singlecell collection (11,914 single-cell test samples spanning 12 tissues, five diseases, and 106 cell types), using disjoint training, validation, and test partitions (§4.5). Compared to supervised fine-tuning baselines, RLVR training yields consistent improvements: accuracy on the held-out immune-cell evaluation set improves from 70.7% to 82.8% (+12.1 pp), rare neuroblastoma recognition rises from 36.1% to 65.0% (+28.9 pp), population-level cell-type classification accuracy increases from 16.8% to 78.0% (+61.2 pp), and COVID-19 disease-state prediction accuracy doubles to 66.2%.

Our main contributions are as follows:

- We introduce RVQ discrete tokenization for single-cell transcriptomics, marking the first application of residual vector quantization to gene expression data. An eight-codebook residual quantizer (with 32 entries each) produces a representation of 10 tokens per cell embedded natively in the LLM vocabulary (§3.2), requiring 60% fewer tokens than text-based approaches (Table 8). Furthermore, empirical analysis (§6) demonstrates that earlier codebooks predominantly encode broader identity levels, whereas later codebooks refine within-lineage variation.
- We develop scCoT-Synth , an evidence-grounded supervision engine that overcomes the coldstart problem of newly added biological tokens without requiring a cross-modal projector. By coupling constraint-conditioned rationale synthesis with a seven-layer verification stack, this engine aligns discrete token representations with gene-level semantics entirely through the language modeling loss.
- We construct a multi-component RLVR reward system with Fact Verification that validates biological claims against actual expression data. This approach addresses the hallucination problem inherent in LLM-native biological tokenization, yielding up to a 4.6 × improvement on population-level tasks (Table 9).

## 2 Related Work

### 2.1 Single-Cell Foundation Models with Continuous Representations

The first generation of single-cell foundation models employed custom transformer architectures operating directly on continuous gene expression data.

scGPT [5] trains a generative transformer on 33 million cells via masked gene expression prediction, thereby supporting multi-task fine-tuning for annotation, integration, and perturbation prediction.

Geneformer [6] utilizes rank-value encoding over 30 million cells to learn network hierarchies through attention weights.

scBERT [12] adapts BERT with binning-based discretization for cell-type annotation.

Subsequent efforts have continued to scale: scFoundation [13] (100M parameters, 50M cells), CellFM [7] (800M parameters, 100M cells, RetNet architecture), and scPRINT-2 [8] (350M cells across 16 organisms).

### 2.2 Language Models for Single-Cell Biology

Aparallel line of research incorporates general-purpose LLMs into single-cell analysis by converting expression data into text.

Cell2Sentence [19] pioneered this direction by transforming expression profiles into ranked gene 'sentences' for GPT-2 fine-tuning, an approach later scaled to 27B parameters [20].

CELLama [21] applies sentence transformers to ranked gene lists with metadata integration.

scMulan [22] encodes genes and expression values as entity-value tuples using multi-task prompting.

LangCell [24] and CellHermes [25] develop cross-modal pretraining frameworks that unify text and expression within shared representation spaces.

InstructCell [27] constructs an instruction-following copilot for single-cell tasks, while CellWhisperer [26] combines Geneformer embeddings with BioBERT via CLIP-like training for chat-based exploration.

Stack [36] trains on 149M cells using tabular attention with in-context learning capabilities.

Although these text-based approaches benefit from pre-trained language understanding, they face fundamental trade-offs. Ranked gene lists discard quantitative precision and scale poorly, as representing even 20 genes requires 40-80 tokens. More critically, text-based representations preclude autoregressive cell generation : the model cannot produce new cell states by generating tokens that map back to expression profiles.

### 2.3 Discrete Tokenization for Biological Data

Vector quantization converts continuous signals into discrete codes that LLMs can process natively.

VQ-VAE [37] introduced the quantized autoencoder framework for images.

Residual Vector Quantization, employed in neural audio codecs [38, 39], applies sequential codebooks to residuals, capturing multi-scale structure through progressive refinement.

In biology, Sun et al. [40] apply geometric byte-pair encoding for protein structure tokenization.

Most relevant to our work, CellTok [31] concurrently demonstrated VQ-VAE discrete tokenization for early-fusion integration with Qwen2.5. They showed that discrete cell tokens enable population-level analyses previously unattainable with text-based approaches. However, CellTok employs a single shared codebook-a design that cannot allocate capacity across biological scales-and produces a 34-token per-cell footprint, which is 3.4 × longer than the representation we propose in §3.2.

### 2.4 Reasoning and Reinforcement Learning for Biological LLMs

Recent work has begun exploring reasoning-enhanced LLMs for single-cell biology.

CellReasoner [29] fine-tunes a 7B LLM with 380 chain-of-thought exemplars for marker-by-marker reasoning during cell-type annotation, thereby achieving strong few-shot performance.

Cell-o1 [30] applies reinforcement learning with batch-level rewards to single-cell reasoning puzzles, demonstrating a 73% improvement over OpenAI's o1 on batch-level annotation accuracy.

OmniCellAgent [41] employs multi-agent orchestration for autonomous single-cell analysis and hypothesis generation.

Beyond the single-cell domain, the RLVR paradigm has been applied to broader biological reasoning.

DeepSeek-R1 [32] demonstrated that verifiable rewards improve LLM reasoning by margins comparable to several months of pretraining scaling.

rbio1 [34] trains scientific reasoning LLMs using biological world models as 'soft verifiers,' setting the state of the art on the PerturbQA benchmark.

VCWorld [42] integrates structured biological knowledge with LLM reasoning for interpretable perturbation prediction.

RVQ-Alpha builds on these developments by combining discrete tokenization (enabling bidirectional generation), Evidence-First reasoning (grounding predictions in gene evidence), and RLVR with a Fact Verification module that validates claims against actual expression data. This integrated approach addresses the hallucination risk inherent in LLM-native biological tokenization.

### 2.5 Perturbation Prediction

Predicting cellular responses to perturbations is a core task for virtual cell models.

CPA [43] learns factorized perturbation embeddings with adversarial disentanglement.

GEARS [44] integrates gene-gene knowledge graphs for multigene perturbation prediction.

Diffusion-based approaches such as Squidiff [45] model perturbation responses through continuous denoising.

RVQ-Alpha addresses perturbation prediction through a distinct mechanism: RVQ tokens enable the model to autoregressively generate post-perturbation cell states, producing discrete token sequences that can be decoded back to expression profiles via the RVQ decoder.

## 3 Method

RVQ-Alpha integrates four components into an end-to-end framework for single-cell transcriptomic reasoning: (i) a residual-quantization cell tokenizer that embeds gene expression into the native token vocabulary of the LLM (§3.2), (ii) a scCoT-Synth pipeline that constructs evidence-first chain-of-thought training data (§3.3), (iii) a three-stage training pipeline progressing from token grounding through multi-task SFT to reinforcement learning (§3.4), and (iv) a multi-teacher distillation and fact-aware reward system that unifies multi-task capabilities while suppressing biological hallucinations (§3.6-3.8). ### 3.2 Residual Quantization for LLM-Native Cell Tokens

Tokenizing cell data for an LLM involves balancing competing requirements.

Aper-cell footprint short enough to fit tens of cells alongside instruction text in one context window demands aggressive compression, whereas preserving the continuum of cellular identity (from broad lineage distinctions to fine-grained functional states) requires expressive capacity.

Athird constraint compounds the first two: cell tokens must enter the LLM through its native token embedding table rather than through a separately learned cross-modal adapter, which would relegate gene expression to a secondary modality and add a trainable bottleneck between cell identity and the reasoning path.

Existing designs fail to optimally resolve this tradeoff.

Text-based encodings such as Cell2Sentence [19] consume 100-500 tokens per cell, thereby precluding population-scale prompts.

Two structural properties follow directly from the residual recursion.

First, each codebook is fitted to the residual left by its predecessors; optimization therefore drives earlier codebooks to explain the highest-variance directions of the latent space, leaving progressively smaller residuals for later codebooks to refine.

Second, this variance-ordered decomposition permits, though does not guarantee, a correspondence with the hierarchical organization of cellular identity, where lineage typically dictates the primary axes of transcriptomic variation and functional states refine variation within lineages.

We therefore state as a testable hypothesis, rather than an architectural property, that earlier codebooks predominantly encode lineage-defining features, whereas later codebooks encode cell-type-specific and state-specific signatures; we validate this empirically in §6.

### 3.3 scCoT-Synth: Evidence-Grounded Supervision Engine for Cold-Start RVQ Alignment

The 256 RVQ tokens introduced during vocabulary expansion carry no pretrained semantics. This cold-start grounding problem compounds the reasoning-format challenge of DeepSeek-R1 [32] with a cross-modal dimension.

scCoT-Synth addresses this issue by requiring every CoT training trajectory to decode RVQ codes into verifiable gene evidence before reasoning. Consequently, the standard next-token prediction loss forces the RVQ embeddings toward the gene-name representations already encoded in the pretrained weights.

The resulting engine produces approximately 600K training samples covering 46 task variants that span the seven task families.

The model must produce verifiable gene evidence E (e.g., 'elevated CD8A , CD8B , GZMB ; absent CD4 ') before any reasoning step R .

Because gene-name tokens in E are predicted from RVQ input positions via the attention mechanism, the cross-entropy loss on these tokens provides a dense supervisory signal that pulls RVQ embeddings toward the pretrained gene-name representations, without requiring any explicit alignment module.

Scaffolded teacher-student synthesis. The constraint is realized through a teacher-student protocol with asymmetric information.

During teacher synthesis , a replaceable LLM backend receives curated gene features (such as expressionranked genes, known markers, active transcription factors, and functional gene modules) alongside c and t , enabling biologically accurate evidence-first reasoning.

A conclusion-blind elicitation protocol prevents the teacher from short-circuiting this process. Although the teacher receives metadata labels for context anchoring, the system prompt constrains it to derive conclusions solely from gene evidence, reasoning as if discovering them for the first time. Deterministic validators flag outputs that reference labels directly. For continued-pretraining data, the teacher regenerates these flagged samples; for CoT data, the flag is recorded as a non-blocking quality signal.

During student training , gene features are stripped entirely, and the input to the student contains only c ⊕ t .

Gene names survive solely in the assistant response (the training target) as evidence claims that the student must learn to predict from the RVQ codes alone.

The gene features thus act as scaffolding : they are present during construction to ensure teacher accuracy and absent during training to force genuine RVQ grounding.

Task-grounded generation at scale. Grounding RVQ tokens requires exposure across various biological granularities, including individual cell profiles, cell populations, and extended expository contexts, because a single granularity would bias the learned representations toward that specific level of description.

scCoT-Synth therefore comprises three generation tiers: (i) cell-level CoT generation across five annotation tasks (cell-type classification, tissue classification, disease classification, gene-name reconstruction, and expression-profile generation); (ii) population-level CoT across five tasks operating on groups of up to ∼ 50 cells; and (iii) audience-diversified continued pretraining drawn from a combinatorial space defined by four audience profiles, nine stylistic templates, and up to 108 variants along four content dimensions (analysis depth, reasoning path, gene organization, and data format).

All tiers share the core design principles of teacher scaffolding and conclusion-blind elicitation, adapted to the output format of each tier.

Even after grounding, supervised fine-tuning alone imposes a ceiling on output quality. The model can only reproduce reasoning patterns present in the training data and cannot self-correct factual or logical errors without outcome-based feedback.

These constraints motivate a three-stage pipeline (Figure 1).

Stage 1 (Continued Pretraining) grounds the new RVQ tokens in the representation space of the language model.

Stage 2 (Multi-Task SFT) teaches structured instruction following and CoT reasoning across seven single-cell task families.

Stage 3 (RLVR via GSPO) refines reasoning quality and factual reliability through verifiable reward signals (§3.7).

Across all three stages, the upstream RVQ encoder f enc remains frozen; only the LLM parameters are updated.

###### Stage 1: Continued Pretraining

The goal is to ground the 256 newly introduced RVQ tokens in the pre-trained representation space of the language model prior to any task-specific supervision.

Vocabulary expansion adds five structural delimiters and 256 RVQ tokens. These are initialized under a diversity-promoting scheme ensuring that the new embeddings are geometrically dispersed rather than clustered in the representation space (full procedure in §3.2).

All parameters of the language model, including the newly added token embeddings, are jointly optimized under a standard next-token prediction objective.

The training data consists of RVQ-text paired descriptions spanning diverse linguistic registers (nine template families; §3.3), which are interleaved with biomedical literature and explicitly cover the top 500 gene names.

Unlike conventional continued pretraining with pure text, Stage 1 data already adopts an instructionresponse format. This enables task-oriented co-exposure : the model simultaneously learns RVQ semantics and the prompt structure of downstream tasks, thereby reducing the distributional gap to Stage 2.

The resulting checkpoint yields a model that maps RVQ tokens to meaningful representations but lacks structured task-following behavior, which motivates the multi-task supervision of Stage 2.

###### Stage 2: Multi-Task SFT

The goal of Stage 2 is to teach the model structured instruction following and chain-of-thought reasoning across seven single-cell task families.

The Stage 1 checkpoint is fine-tuned on the full scCoT-Synth corpus. Each training example carries a task-family annotation that selects the appropriate input formatting and loss masking for one of the seven task families.

Stage 2 performs full fine-tuning of the language-model backbone while keeping the upstream RVQ encoder frozen.

###### Stage 3: RLVR via Group Sequence Policy Optimization

The goal of Stage 3 is to refine reasoning quality and factual reliability beyond the SFT ceiling through reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards.

We adopt Group Sequence Policy Optimization (GSPO) [46], which operates at the sequence level rather than the token level (§3.5).

Rollout prompts are drawn from the same task families as in SFT, and group-normalized advantages (§3.5) provide a per-task baseline that adapts to heterogeneous reward distributions.

The Stage 3 checkpoint constitutes the final RVQ-Alpha model evaluated in §4.

### 3.7 Ontology-Grounded Reward Design

Ontology-aware semantic evaluation. Exact-match rewards are ill-suited to biological cell type prediction, where valid names coexist at different levels of the Cell Ontology [50] hierarchy.

B cell' and naive B cell' are both correct descriptions of the same population at different specificity levels; T helper cell' and CD4-positive T cell' name the same entity in different nomenclatures.

Under exact-match scoring, a model that correctly identifies a naive B cell but outputs the parent term B cell' receives zero reward (identical to a prediction of neuron'), collapsing the entire ontological hierarchy into a binary signal that actively penalizes valid predictions.

The reward must therefore capture graded semantic similarity anchored to ontological distance, rather than reducing biological correctness to string identity.

Afrozen LLM judge addresses this by evaluating the semantic relationship between prediction ˆ y and ground truth y ∗ with explicit Cell Ontology awareness. The rubric below defines the reward-side answer judge , which is distinct from the post-training offline evaluation judge used to report accuracy (§4.5); its category schema and multi-sample aggregation are properties of the training-time reward system only.

The reward-side answer judge classifies responses into seven graded categories reflecting positions on the ontology graph, ranging from Exact Match and More Specific (valid sub-type) through Less Specific (valid ancestor) to Wrong Branch , Vague/Evasive , Wrong Lineage , and Unrelated , spanning a continuous score in [ -1, 1 ] (scoring intervals are detailed in Appendix A.4).

### 3.8 Fact-Aware Reinforcement Learning

The ontology-grounded reward described above ensures prediction correctness, but a correct answer produced through flawed reasoning (such as citing fabricated gene markers or hallucinating pathway associations) represents a distinct failure mode that the answer judge cannot detect.

Hallucination-accuracy trade-off. Standard hallucination-penalized RLVR using a count-based ratio R = R task + α · R hallu suffers from two failure modes: (i) redundancy-induced dilution , where the policy outputs many correct but uninformative sentences to inflate the denominator and suppress the apparent hallucination rate; and (ii) penalty-induced conservatism , where a large α drives the policy toward terse outputs that sacrifice the multi-step reasoning required for biological inference.

An effective fact reward must therefore weight claims by their informational importance to defeat dilution, while modulating its influence based on the current task competence of the model to avoid premature conservatism.

Inspired by Baichuan-M3 [49], RVQ-Alpha employs a saliency-weighted, dynamically gated factverification reward adapted for single-cell biological reasoning.

Saliency-weighted fact reward. Each reasoning chain is decomposed into independently verifiable atomic claims, which are evaluated against domain-specific knowledge bases covering cell-type markers, pathway memberships, and disease-gene associations, and subsequently classified as SUPPORTED, REFUTED, or UNCERTAIN.

### 4.1 Base Model

RVQ-Alpha uses a single base LLM across all training stages and task families: Qwen3-4B-Thinking2507 [51] (4.0,B parameters, V text = 151,941), extended with Nc · C = 8 × 32 = 256 RVQ single-cell vocabulary slots, five structural delimiter tokens, and the top-500 gene name tokens (tokenizer details in §3.2).

### 4.5 Evaluation Protocol

LLM-as-judge for post-training evaluation. All post-training numbers in this paper are produced by an offline evaluation judge that is implemented and run separately from the training-time reward judge (§3.7) and the training-time monitoring judge. These layers share neither a scoring scheme nor aggregation logic. Following Zheng et al. [33], the offline judge is DeepSeek-chat (DeepSeek-AI, dense) accessed through the DeepSeek cloud API. For each prediction, it emits a task-aware semantic relation label together with a continuous semantic score in [ 0, 1 ] ; this scalar is then thresholded to form a binary accuracy. For the single-cell cell-type task, we adopt a conservative operating point of τ = 0.8. Under this threshold, exact matches and strong ontology-consistent refinements are counted as correct, whereas broader lineage-adjacent or merely partially related predictions are not credited as fine-grained biological identification. For population tasks, the offline judge is applied at the per-cell level with task-specific prompt families (cell type, tissue, disease state), and per-cell scores are aggregated into sample-level summaries at a grain that depends on the reported metric (per-cellthresholded accuracy vs. per-sample mean-score correctness; see the Population evaluation paragraph below). Thresholds are task-dependent: we follow the operating points of τ = 0.75 for cell-type and tissue-type populations and the stricter τ = 0.85 for the disease-state population, consistent with the per-task figure captions in App. A.1-App. A.2.

The offline evaluation judge and the training-time reward judge are operationally separate judge stacks, implemented in distinct codepaths with different endpoints and prompt configurations, and served by different model families (reward judge: Qwen3-30B-A3B, Alibaba, MoE; offline evaluation judge: DeepSeek-chat, DeepSeek-AI, dense). ### 4.6 Baselines

RVQ-Alpha emits free-form natural-language cell-type labels end-to-end, graded by an LLM judge (§4.5). The primary numerical comparison is established through a two-layer rule. Protocol compatibility requires that a candidate produce a categorical label through its native, published inference protocol so that the same LLM judge can grade it without constructing a new benchmark bridge. Capacity fairness in the primary comparison additionally restricts that set to systems whose parameter scale is close enough to our 4B backbone for an accuracy gap to be interpretable as a method gap rather than a capacity gap. Three baselines satisfy both layers:

1. LangCell [24]: a Geneformer + PubMedBERT dual-encoder that performs zero-shot cell-type annotation via nearest-prototype scoring over cell-type names.
2. Cell-o1 [30]: a Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct model trained with SFT and GRPO that emits natural-language labels with reasoning chains.
3. C2S-Scale-Gemma2-2B [20]: a Gemma-2-2B model fine-tuned on cell sentences that emits a label string under the same text-generation protocol.

Other candidates anticipated by the single-cell community fall outside the primary comparison for diverse reasons rather than a single criterion. scGPT [5] and Geneformer [6] require an external protocol bridge before our judge can grade them; CellFM [7] requires task-specific fine-tuning for cell-type annotation; and C2S-Scale-Gemma2-27B [20] is protocol-compatible but, at 6.8 × the size of our backbone, falls outside the capacity-fairness layer and is therefore reported separately in §5.2 as a capacity-scaled reference rather than as a primary comparison point. App. A.6 documents the per-system rationale.

### 4.7 LCAD Computation

Computing LCAD (Eq. 24) requires mapping free-text predictions to Cell Ontology identifiers. We use the deterministic five-tier cascade summarized in §4.5; the only LLM-derived signal enters at Tier 4 and is bounded by a static lookup table whose maximum value is 6.0, so any reported LCAD &gt; 6 originates from the ontology graph rather than the fallback. Across the eight evaluation datasets, the ontology graph (Tiers 1-3) resolves 49.6% of LCAD computations, with per-dataset coverage ranging from 22.6% on the lowest-coverage corpus to 84.1% on the highest, and leaves no unresolved predictions. The per-dataset breakdown and the full cascade specification appear in App. A.7.

### 6.2 Semantic Understanding of RVQ Tokens

Evidence for the hierarchy hypothesis. Section 3.2 hypothesizes that the variance-ordered decomposition produced by residual quantization exhibits a coarse-to-fine correspondence with the hierarchical organization of cellular identity. Figure 8 supports this hypothesis: a directed-graph analysis over 100K class-balanced cells shows that earlier codebooks (CB-0-CB-2) predominantly partition cells at broader identity levels (major cell groups, representative subtypes, shared cell states), whereas later codebooks (CB-3-CB-4) refine within-lineage variation into functional programs and pathway markers. We note that this serves as evidence rather than formal proof; the decomposition allows, but does not guarantee, such an alignment, as residual codebooks are trained symmetrically.

Semantic structure of predictions. To assess whether the LLM develops a biological understanding of RVQtokens rather than merely memorizing mappings, we examine the semantic confusion structure of its predictions alongside the corresponding embedding-space organization in Appendix A.1 (Figures 10 and 11). The confusion matrix shows that most off-diagonal mass concentrates between ontologically adjacent cell types rather than across unrelated lineages, confirming that RVQ tokenization preserves the local semantic structure of gene expression. The companion t-SNE visualization shows that biologically related cell types cluster together in the RVQ embedding space of the LLM. Neuronal cells form a distinct cluster separated from immune and epithelial lineages, while immune subtypes (T cells, NK, Myeloid) exhibit substantial overlap, which is consistent with their shared ontological proximity. Based on these complementary views, we hypothesize three properties regarding the underlying interaction between RVQ and reasoning: (1) attention over RVQ positions tracks biologically relevant gene modules; (2) attention shifts across codebook positions depending on the biological property the model evaluates (lineage versus functional state); and (3) codebook combinations unseen during training continue to yield biologically coherent predictions. We state these explicitly as hypotheses, leaving direct evidence from attention patterns to future work. Companion confusion matrices for tissue type and disease state, along with an alluvial diagram of misclassification flows, are provided in the same appendix (Figures 12-14).

## 7 Limitations

RVQ-Alpha presents several limitations that provide directions for future work:

###### RVQ information loss.

Compressing ∼ 20,000 gene dimensions into 8 discrete tokens inherently discards fine-grained expression information. Although this representation is sufficient for cell type classification, the loss of information may degrade performance on tasks that require precise quantitative expression values (e.g., differential expression fold-change prediction).

###### Evaluation scope.

We report results on a held-out pool of eight ScBank datasets (12 tissues, five diseases, 106 cell types; §4.4). This dataset-level held-out protocol is less rigorous than benchmark-wide leave-one-tissue-out or donor-level out-of-distribution (OOD) evaluation. Furthermore, generalization to other dataset compositions, non-human organisms, and rare pathological states remains to be validated.

###### Computational cost.

Each RLVR step issues multiple LLM-as-judge queries per generated response (three samples per response in our setup, adding approximately one second of latency per query). Consequently, the judge dominates the wall-clock time of large-scale RLVR training. Future work could address this by distilling the judge into a lightweight classifier.

###### Gene name dependency.

During training, Feature-Injection SFT relies on the topK gene names. At inference time, without gene injection, the model must rely entirely on learned RVQ semantics; this dependence can degrade performance for very rare cell types that have limited training coverage.

###### Population scale.

Due to context length constraints, current population-level tasks are limited to 3-48 cells per sample. Scaling to atlas-level populations (thousands of cells) requires efficient attention mechanisms or hierarchical summarization techniques.

###### Disease state discrimination.

For disease state classification, the semantic confusion matrix (Figure 14) reveals that four out of five disease classes collapse into the healthy class. This indicates that the current reward signal lacks the contrast necessary to train disease discrimination under severe class imbalance.

###### Baseline comparison scope.

The cross-system comparison (Figure 5; Table 16, App. A.8) covers the three primary baselines selected by the two-stage criteria of §4.6 (protocol compatibility and capacity fairness). We omit other candidates from the primary comparison for various reasons: scGPT, Geneformer, and CellFM fail the protocolcompatibility criterion (because they yield encoder-only outputs or require task adaptation), whereas C2S-Scale-27B is protocol-compatible but exceeds the capacity limit of our 4B backbone. We discuss both groups in §5.2 and App. A.6 without numerical evaluation. Finally, CellTok [31] cannot be compared until its official weights are released.

###### Baseline training-data overlap.

The training corpus of C2S-Gemma2-2B is not fully documented [20]. Thus, we cannot verify whether any of the eight evaluation datasets appeared in its training data. This is particularly concerning for dataset D103, where C2S-Gemma2-2B achieves 96.0% accuracy at pass@8. We advise readers to consider this caveat when interpreting the per-dataset comparisons in Table 16 (App. A.8).

## 8 Conclusion

We presented RVQ-Alpha, an end-to-end framework that bridges single-cell transcriptomics and large language models through three key components: RVQ discrete tokenization, Evidence-First FeatureInjection SFT, and RLVR with fact verification. By converting continuous gene expression profiles into discrete tokens and embedding them directly into the LLM vocabulary, RVQ-Alpha achieves bidirectional unification, enabling both the interpretation and generation of cell representations within a single model.

Empirical results demonstrate that RLVR with domain-specific verifiable rewards yields a 4.6 × performance improvement on population classification, a 28.9 pp increase in accuracy for rare cell types, and a 12.1 pp gain in out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization (Tables 8 and 9). The fact verification module is critical for grounding model outputs in actual expression data, thereby reducing hallucinations and enhancing generalization (Table 10).

RVQ-Alpha establishes a foundation for AI Virtual Cells, providing a scalable, interpretable, and generative substrate for multi-modal biological intelligence. Future work will extend this framework to support cross-species generalization, atlas-scale populations, and the integration of additional modalities (e.g., spatial transcriptomics and chromatin accessibility).

Six sequential checks-template regurgitation ( -0.5), answer overlong ( -0.2 gradual), marker stuffing ( -0.5 gradual, threshold exceeds w base by design), answer repetition ( -0.15 gradual), truncation without EOS ( -0.3), and answer leakage into reasoning ( -0.15).

Akey design principle: the marker stuffing penalty ( -0.5) intentionally exceeds w base = 0.3, ensuring that even a semantically correct answer with marker stuffing produces a net negative reward.

### A.6 Supplementary F: Baselines Not Included in the Primary Numerical Comparison

This subsection records, for the four candidates that the single-cell community might expect, the persystem reason each is omitted from the primary numerical comparison defined in §4.6. The omissions do not share a single cause: two arise from the protocol-compatibility layer (an external bridge would be required), one from a task-adaptation requirement, and one from the capacity-fairness layer of the primary comparison. We separate them explicitly so that no exclusion is misread as a generic 'protocol-incompatible' verdict.

Protocol-bridge cases (scGPT, Geneformer). scGPT [5] and Geneformer [6] produce cell embeddings rather than labels. A categorical prediction therefore requires either fine-tuning a classification head on the target cell types or attaching a nearest-prototype head over cell-type-name embeddings from an external text encoder; both routes introduce a head-choice or text-encoder confound that the native-protocol baselines do not carry. LangCell is included in the main evaluation because it provides its own published protocol bridge (PubMedBERT + Geneformer), giving an end-to-end pipeline that the LLM judge can grade directly without an additional bridge of our own construction.

Task-adaptation case (CellFM). CellFM [7] requires fine-tuning the 80M variant on the target labels for cell-type annotation; the 800M variant supports zero-shot inference only for gene-level tasks (gene function, GO multiclass), not cell-type labels [7]. The native release therefore does not provide an end-to-end cell-type label under the same conditions as the primary baselines.

Capacity-scaled, protocol-compatible reference (C2S-Scale-Gemma2-27B). C2S-Scale-Gemma227B [20] satisfies the protocol-compatibility layer: it produces labels under the same text-generation protocol as C2S-Scale-Gemma2-2B and has been publicly available since October 2025. Its omission from the primary comparison is a capacity-fairness decision, not a protocol decision. At 27B parameters it is 6.8 × larger than our single-cell backbone (Qwen3-4B-Thinking-2507, 4.0B parameters); the parameter-matched member of the same family is C2S-Scale-Gemma2-2B (2.0B), already included in the main evaluation. C2S-Scale-Gemma2-27B (C2S-Scale-27B for short) is therefore reported separately in §5.2 as a capacity-scaled reference rather than as a primary comparison point.

### A.7 Supplementary G: LCAD Resolution Cascade and Per-Dataset Coverage

This subsection expands the deterministic five-tier name resolution cascade summarized in §4.5 and reports the per-dataset coverage statistics referenced from §4.7. Because model outputs and groundtruth labels use heterogeneous naming conventions (e.g., 'Treg' vs. 'regulatory T cell', 'CD8+ T cell' vs. 'CD8-positive, alpha-beta T cell'), the cascade resolves names to CL identifiers before any graph distance is computed.

1. Tier 0 - Exact match : if the predicted and ground-truth strings are identical, LCAD = 0.
2. Tier 1 - Direct Cell Ontology lookup : both names are looked up in the name-to-identifier index built from the OBO Foundry Cell Ontology release ( cl.obo ) recorded in §4.5; on success, LCAD is computed by finding the lowest common ancestor on the is-a DAGand summing the shortest-path distances from each node to that ancestor.
3. Tier 2 - Synonym mapping : a human-curated synonym table maps common variants (e.g., 'Treg' → 'regulatory T cell') to canonical names, then retries Tier 1.
4. Tier 3 - Deterministic normalization : a deterministic string-rewriting normalizer applies six rule groups-CD-marker normalization, Greek-letter expansion, tissue/layer prefix stripping, '-like' suffix removal, dataset-code removal, and whitespace collapse-and re-attempts Tier 1.
5. Tier 4 - Semantic-relation fallback : when Tiers 0-3 all fail, the semantic-relation label assigned by the post-training evaluation judge is mapped through a static lookup table whose maximum value is 6.0. A Tier 1 lookup that succeeds but yields no valid LCA (e.g., disconnected components in the local subgraph) also falls through to this tier.

The static lookup table caps Tier-4 output at 6.0, so any reported LCAD &gt; 6 value originates from the ontology graph used by Tiers 1-3 rather than the fallback.
