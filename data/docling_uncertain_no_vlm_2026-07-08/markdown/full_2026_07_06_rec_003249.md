## Machine learning approaches enable the discovery of therapeutics across domains

Prabal Chhibbar 1,3 and Jishnu Das 1,2

1 Centre for Systems Immunology, Department of Immunology, School of Medicine, University of Pittsburgh, Pittsburgh, PA 15213, USA;  2 Department of Computational and Systems Biology, School of Medicine, University of Pittsburgh, Pittsburgh, PA 15213, USA;  3 Integrative Systems Biology PhD Program, School of Medicine, University of Pittsburgh, Pittsburgh, PA 15213, USA

Multi-modal  datasets  have  grown  exponentially  in  the  last decade.  This  has  created  an  enormous  demand  for  machine learning models  that can predict  complex  outcomes  by leveraging  cellular,  molecular,  and  humoral  profiles.  Corresponding inference of mechanisms can help to uncover new therapeutic targets. Here, we discuss how biological principles guide the design of predictive models and how interpretable machine learning can lead to novel mechanistic insights. We provide  descriptions  of  multiple  learning  techniques  and how suited  they  are  to  domain  adaptations.  Finally,  we  talk about  broad  learning  capabilities  of  foundation  models  on large datasets and whether they can be used to provide meaningful inference about biological datasets.

### INTRODUCTION

Next-generation sequencing has revolutionized molecular biology by enabling the rapid sequencing of DNA, RNA, and proteins at different resolutions. This technology  allows  for the parallel sequencing of millions of DNA fragments, providing comprehensive  insights  into  genomics,  epigenomics,  transcriptomics  and proteomics. 1 These  advances  have  been  applied  to  understand the  molecular  variations  present  across  populations  and  individuals to precisely design therapeutic interventions. 2 These include understanding  the  genetics  associated  with  variable  efficacy  of drugs  like  warfarin 3 and  designing  therapies  by  studying  immunogenicity of unique sets of genetic alterations that arise in cancer patient's tumor cells (neo-antigens). 4 However, conventional statistical  approaches used for deconvolving information do not account  for  differences  in  data  modalities,  highly  correlated  variables in datasets, and large numbers  of irrelevant features, which  make  the  analyses  and  integration  of  high-dimensional -omic datasets challenging.

Various machine learning and deep learning algorithms have been developed especially for high-dimensional datasets with increasing applications in molecular biology. The design of precise molecular therapies relies on this information to find specific cellular targets. These considerations have motivated the aggregation of cellular profiles from different studies to form global atlases such as the human cell atlas. 5 Coupled with these, advanced machine learning methods have been developed to learn multi-modal and, in some cases, dy- namic relationships from these datasets to specifically target relevant biological systems. These approaches include scalable and fast annotation  of  different  cell  types,  deconvolution  of  single-cell  profiles from  bulk  datasets,  and  predicting  the  drug-induced  changes  to gene expression. 6 In addition, in silico methods have been used to prioritize targets for different molecular therapies across data modalities. The success of these computational methods depends on usability, scalability, and adaptability to the specific biological context. The successful applications of computational learning systems require a sufficient  understanding  of  their  nuances  for  their  incorporation with biological information to design better therapies. In this review, we  highlight  different  machine  learning  techniques  and  concepts, along with their applications in relevant fields. We provide a conceptual breakdown of several algorithms and discuss fundamental questions  regarding  the  explainability  of  machine  learning  algorithms, including advantages and limitations (Figure 1).

### BUILDING BLACK BOXES: SOFTWARE FOR BIOLOGICAL PREDICTIVE MODELING

The primary function of learning algorithms is to learn the relationship between the observed data and a response variable for making predictions on unlabeled data points. Most deep learning algorithms are black boxes and we do not understand the reasoning behind their prediction  decisions.  In  this  section,  we  highlight  several  learning methods  and  how  their  predictions  have  been  used  in  multiple contexts.

#### Artificial neural networks

An artificial neural network (ANN) 7 is the primary deep learning architecture. ANNs mimic the brain in that it is a network of information-processing nodes called neurons. ANNs are multi-layered, where the first layer takes the input, and the final layer gives the result depending on the prediction task. ANNs can be trained on large quantitative datasets to predict a binary outcome (classification) or a continuous outcome (regression). Theoretically, neural networks  can  approximate  different  mathematical  relationships, and different neural network architectures of varying complexities have  become  widely  popular  across  domains. 8 They  have  been applied in various therapeutic contexts but we shall discuss the prediction of peptide and major histocompatibility complexes (MHCs) as an important case study. The MHC binds and presents a protein fragment or peptide to trigger a T cell immune response. The polymorphic nature and structural promiscuity of the binding regions of  the  human  leukocyte  antigen  (HLA)  enables  the  presentation of a large repertoire of peptides. Characterizing this immunopeptidome has broad applications in vaccine design, 9 cancer immunotherapy, 4 and  understanding  autoimmune  disorders. 10 Notably, cancers have immune evasion strategies that are vulnerable to the recognition of altered peptides by MHC. 4 The discovery of these neo-antigens is the basic principle behind the development of cancer vaccines. 11 NetMHCPan is a web server that uses specific machine  learning  strategies  to  predict  peptide-MHC  interactions. 12

In  more  detail,  NetMHCPan  makes  these  predictions  by  quantifying critical sequence features such as the presence of certain motifs  on  the  MHC  sequence  and  the  sequence  composition  of  the peptide  binding  core  and  training  an  ANN  on  them.  The  ability of ANNs to be trained on quantitative target data makes it ideally suited  for  constructing  sequence  to  affinity  models  like  the  peptide-MHC  system.  Additionally,  ANNs  can  also  capture the higher-order correlations, which are important for modeling positional correlations in receptor-ligand interactions. 13,14 Subsequent algorithmic  adaptations  of  NetMHCPAN  have  helped  to  predict MHC-bound peptides across the HLA-allele spectrum. 15-17

Another tool for prediction of peptide-MHC interactions is MHCFlurry. MHCFlurry is an open source software package that can predict peptide-MHC interactions using an ensemble of ANN models for each MHC allele. 18 Ensembling is a technique where multiple models are combined to improve the overall predictive performance  compared  with  individual  models. 19 The  idea  is  that,  by aggregating multiple weak or strong learners, we can reduce variance, bias, or both, leading to better generalization. The subsequent development of the ANN architecture has led to data-dependent adaptations  of  the  model.  In  the  subsequent  subsections,  we  discuss these specialized deep learning models and their applications in molecular therapies.

#### Recurrent neural networks

Recurrent neural networks (RNNs) are a type of ANN that are designed  to  handle  sequential  data  and  are  adept  at  preserving  the importance of inherent order in the input. In a regular ANN, inputs are processed independent of previous inputs. RNNs have a memory for  past  inputs  in  a  sequence  and  can  also  process  sequences  of different  lengths  as  compared  with  fixed  size  inputs  for  ANNs. RNNs can also recognize that earlier parts of the sequence influence later parts. For example, in a sentence, the meaning of a word often depends on previous words. ANNs treat inputs independently and thus cannot capture such relationships. Due to the aforementioned advantages, RNNs can be used for effectively modeling biological sequences and glean critical insights.

Hill et al. 20 used RNNs for deciphering the RNA protein coding potential  by  training  the  model  on  mRNA  transcripts  to  predict  the protein-coding potential of newly identified RNA  transcripts. Feature-based methods are limited by current scientific knowledge, but  deep  learning  methods  like  RNNs  can  use  the  RNA  sequence alone to figure out important sequence patterns that correlate with protein coding potential. Methods like RNN can be used for harvesting  new  biological  insights  from  the  rising  flood  of  sequencing data. 20 RNNs have also been used for discovering transcription factor  (TF)  binding  sites  in  the  DNA  sequence, 20,21 but  we  shall  talk more about this challenging task in the next section, where ANNs used for capturing information in images were adapted to better understand gene regulation.

#### Convolutional neural networks

Convolutional  neural  networks  (CNNs)  are  used  extensively  for processing  grid-like  data  such  as  images.  They  use  a  multi-layer structure where each layer has a specific function. The namesake convolutional layer uses small filters to detect edges, textures, and patterns. The output of these filters is transformed using specialized functions and the pooling layer reduces the input size while keeping all the important features for faster downstream computation. After  all  the  mentioned  processing, a  conventional  ANN processed the output to make the final predictions. 22 Convolutions can also be used to detect patterns in high-dimensional biological datasets. This has been used to detect TF footprints within regions of accessible chromatin in a cell-type-specific manner. This is important to determine the regulatory determinants of gene expression, as TF motifs  are  general  consensus  sequences  whose  corresponding enrichment does not imply TF binding. TFs will selectively bind a  small  fraction  of  potential sites  in  a  highly  cell-context-specific manner. ChromBPNet 23 uses a CNN to model unstranded, baseresolution assay for transposase-accessible chromatin sequencing (ATAC-seq)  or  DNase  sequencing  cleavage profiles in 1-kb genomic segments as a function of local regulatory DNA sequence context. The predictions are used to prioritize specific TF motifs in genomic  sequences. Importantly, context-specific TF  binding predictions correlate with genomic localization of disease-associated  variants  that  frequently  disrupt  TF  occupancy  and  gene expression. 24-26

#### Representation learning

Conventional learning  approaches  rely  on  manually  designed  features for prediction, but this is time consuming and severely limits applications  to  well-annotated  fields.  However,  we  can  use  neural networks to transform the original data into numerical representations that  best  capture  the  most  important  patterns in  images, sequences and other data types without manual feature augmentation. This is known as representation learning.

Although  the  representations  are  generated  in  an  unsupervised manner,  they  can  be  used  as  features  for  training  classifiers  for different tasks. Sliding window interaction grammar 27 (SWING), a representation learning-based method, uses previous work done to extract semantic latent features from documents 28 to convert a biochemically encoded interaction language associated with biological systems. SWING was used effectively in predicting peptide-MHC interactions and the impact of missense variants on specific proteinprotein interactions (PPIs). The latter provides variant effect prediction at a molecular resolution, which can be crucial in devising novel therapeutic  strategies.  Discovery  that  a  population  variant  in  the membrane protein CCR5 conferred resistance to the HIV-1 by disrupting  its  interaction  with  the  viral  envelope  glycoprotein  led  to the development of Maraviroc as a US Food and Drug Administration-approved treatment for HIV-1 that functions by blocking the interface for this interaction. 29,30 The structural information required to solve this problem is extremely sparse, but the effective use of representation learning could impute important information from just the sequences.

#### Deep learning methods for graphs

Biological systems are better understood as an interconnected web of molecular  entities  exerting  different  effects  in  a  combinatorial manner.  These  systems  can  be  mathematically  represented  using the graph data structure. Graphs are widely used in computer science to model relationships between objects and consist of a set of vertices called nodes and connections between these nodes known as edges. The  human  protein  interactome  can  be  represented  as  a  graph, where different proteins are the nodes and binary interactions between them are the edges in the graph. Since biological graphs represent real-world entities, they are more commonly called biological networks.  The  network  representation  of  living  systems  becomes critical in designing therapies to understand the global effects of molecular perturbations and the efficacy of the intervention. As convolutions can capture local information within an image to generate numerical  representations  to learn important  patterns, similar methods  can  be  applied  to  networks  to  understand  local  patterns around a node's neighborhood and their importance to the overall network.  Therefore,  representation  learning  architectures  specifically  made  for  networks  can  generate  numerical  representations for  nodes,  edges,  and  the  entire  network.  These  representations can be used for  modeling the effect  of  perturbations  on  a  system, which is critical in understanding disease pathology as demonstrated by Yeh et al., 31 where Perturb-seq screens identified perturbations in PTPN1 and ACTR8 in sensitizing ovarian cancer cells to T cell and natural  killer  cell  cytotoxicity.  Knowledge  of  such  perturbations, which help cancer cells to evade immunity, can be used to develop effective  cancer  immunotherapies.  The  method  GEARS  applied network  representation  learning  on  cellular  perturbation  profiles to determine the transcriptional outcomes of the cell after perturbation.  GEARS incorporates the gene co-expression network (nodes, genes;  edges,  correlation  in  gene  expression  across  samples)  and gene  ontology  network  (nodes,  gene;  edges,  relationships  determining  the  molecular,  biological,  and  localized  function  of  the gene) to model the effects of a perturbation relying on key intuitions that  genes  that  share  similar  expression  patterns  should  likely respond  similarly  to  external  perturbations  and  genes  involved  in similar pathways should impact the expression of similar genes after perturbation. 32 GEARS generates  the  aggregated  numerical  representation  for  the  genes  by  using  graph  neural  networks  (GNNs). As mentioned, GNNs help to iteratively aggregate feature information from a node's local neighborhoods into a numerical representation. 33 Methods like GEARS can leverage learned information from biological  networks  to  infer  multi-gene  perturbation  outcomes. Furthermore, GEARS can guide the design of new screens by identifying perturbations that maximize information gained and minimize experimental  cost.  Graph  representation  learning  is  also  useful  in meaningfully  integrating  different  biological  networks  (co-expression, gene regulatory, PPIs networks, etc.). 34

The interconnectedness in a network is helpful in visualizing longand  short-range  relationships  between  different  entities.  There  is an inherent geometry within networks that is absent in sequential and grid-like representations of data. It can also be explained as a lack of fixed structure present in languages and images. Understanding  and  learning  these  geometric  patterns  can  be  beneficial  in increasing  performance  biological  network  deep  learning  models. This is known as geometric learning. 35 PINNACLE, which uses geometric learning, is a method for providing cell-type-specific context to  protein  representations  and  interactions. 36 The  context-aware profiling of PPIs using geometric learning can help in the systematic discovery of differential mutation-rewired PPIs to guide tumor-specific  therapeutic  interventions. 37 This is  crucial  in  deciphering  the mutation-induced changes in PPIs in cancer and dependence of tumor on these changes. This has been demonstrated in the context of BTK inhibitor resistance mutations that disable the kinase activity while promoting physical interactions with other kinases to sustain B cell receptor signaling and promote chronic lymphocytic leukemia. 38

#### Contrastive learning

The numerical representations generated by different methods can also be used for precisely differentiating between similar and dissimilar data points. This is by informing the representation generating process to contrast the representations proportionate to the degree of dissimilarity between two data points. This process is known as contrastive  learning. 39 This  concept  has  been  recently  applied  in the  development  of  SCimilarity, 40 a  human-cell-atlas-level  model for scalable search of similar human cells. The method queries tens of millions of cells across hundreds of studies to query for similar cellular states across the human body, providing a tool for generating biological insights from the human cell atlas. SCimilarity uses supervised and unsupervised learning principles to represent the expression profiles of matching cell types closer together in the latent representation space and encourage the model to preserve the variation from the input expression profiles, capturing subtler differences in expression pattern within cells of the same type.

### ML POTTER AND THE CHAMBER OF SECRETS: INTERPRETABILITY AND INFERENCE FOR BETTER THERAPIES

Biological domain adaptability drives the choice of prediction algorithms, but, in engineering novel therapeutic interventions, machine learning models can provide meaningful inference to prioritize reliable targets. Inference-based interpretable machine learning models are different from black box models, where the reasoning used by the algorithm in making decisions is human understandable (Figure 2). Interpretable models also comply with the biologist-in-loop model, as  it  confers  confidence  to  a  model  whose  reasoning  has  been confirmed by a domain expert. 41 There are two major interpretable machine learning techniques used to explain prediction in computational biology applications. Models that are naturally interpretable are known as interpretable-by-design models. 42

Although simple, linear models have been applied to solve complex biological problems. This can be illustrated by studying the model behind CellOracle. CellOracle 43 infers base gene regulatory networks (GRNs), elucidating the broad regulatory relationships between gene and TFs from scATAC-seq datasets. It uses a linear model to confer cell-type or context specificity to the GRN by modeling the expression  of  a  target  gene  as  the  sum  of  the  weighted  expression  of  its TFs. The inferred weights measure the amount of shift in the target gene's expression caused by a shift in the expression of a particular TF. Using  chain  rule on  linear equations, CellOracle  enables modeling the effect of a perturbation beyond the immediate neighborhood  in  the  GRN.  CellOracle  has  been  very  successful  across biological contexts specifically in defining the state transition of neutrophils  dependent  on  the  perturbation  of  CEBPA  expression  in sepsis. 44 However, capturing just the linear signal from high-dimensional datasets does not explain all the variability and causal factors. Significant latent factor interaction discovery and exploration (SLIDE) 45 is  a  first-in-class  statistical  machine  learning  approach that  uses  a  latent  factor  regression  approach  to  move  beyond  the identification of correlates and biomarkers to the inference of putative  biological  mechanisms  underlying  outcomes  of  interest  from high-dimensional -omic datasets. Additionally, it captures the nonlinear structure within the datasets as networks to push explainability beyond linear structure through a series of rigorous statistical guarantees that enable it to identify significant interacting latent factors underlying outcomes. SLIDE has been applied on a range of multimodal  datasets  from  single  cell  to  spatial  to  identify  mechanisms of disease in autoimmune and infectious disease. 45-48

#### Models with post hoc interpretability

The second class of interpretability in machine learning models is post hoc explanations, which are flexible and typically model agnostic due to their application after the design and training of a prediction model. In the mentioned case of ChromBPNet, a post hoc explainer was used to select genomic features most important to the prediction. Particularly, the explainer used in ChromBPNet is DeepLIFT/ DeepSHAP, 49 which estimates the contribution of individual bases in the accessible chromatin  peaks  to the model's prediction. DeepLIFT  is  a  method  that  affords  explainability  by  propagating the error back to individual nodes in the network to every feature in the input to quantify their contribution in the final prediction. 50 Post hoc explanation uncovered a bias in predicting the TF motifs toward the sequence preferences of the particular enzymes used to profile chromatin accessibility.

Another method for post hoc explanations is Shapley values. Shapley values come from game theory and measure the contribution of each feature to the output prediction. This is done by comparing all the possible  subset  of  features  and  measuring  how  much  each  feature changes the model's output when included or removed. Across all the  possible  scenarios,  the  contributions  are  averaged  to  assign feature importance. 51

SCARLink can impute the gene expression from chromatin accessibility using Poisson regression and uses a Shapley value analysis for post hoc explanation to identify cell-type-specific gene enhancers. 52 Importantly,  the  cell-type  enhancers  prioritized  using  SHAP  on the SCARLink model were validated by promoter capture Hi-C assay and were also enriched with disease variants and expression quantitative trait loci.

### GOOD THERAPIES NEED A STRONG FOUNDATION: ARE FOUNDATION MODELS THE NEXT FRONTIER IN COMPUTATIONAL BIOLOGICAL DISCOVERY?

The above sections discussed inference- and prediction-based machine learning models targeted toward specific biological problems. A popular new class of learning models is being used for pan-context biological discovery, which learn on compendium-level datasets in an unsupervised manner and can be slightly retrained to adapt to multiple supervised predictive tasks.  These models are foundation models  and  use  a  class of  learning  known  as  self-supervised learning. 53,54 In this section, we define essential vocabulary used in the foundation model space along with domains where these models have become popular. We also discuss how explainable these models and potential caveats to their widespread application.

#### Self-supervised learning

Self-supervised learning defines a pretext task based on unlabeled inputs  to  produce  descriptive  and  intelligible  representations.  For example, in natural language, a common objective for self-supervised learners is to mask a word in the text and predict surrounding words. This objective helps the model to capture relationships among words in  the  text  without  the  need  for  any  labels.  Unlike  supervised learning, self-supervised learning can learn from vast amounts of unlabeled data. 55

#### Transformers

Foundation  models  use  the  transformer  architecture, 56 which  are self-supervised  learners.  Transformers  have  an  encoder-decoder structure, where the encoder maps an input sequence of symbol representations  to  a  sequence  of  continuous  representations.  The decoder then generates an output sequence of symbols one element at a time. Simply, the encoder reads the input and extracts meaningful  information,  and  the  decoder  creates  a  meaningful  response based on the encoded information. At each step, the model is auto regressive,  using  the  previously  generated  symbols  as  additional input when generating the next. The transformer does not sequentially  read  the  input  but  instead  examines  all  positions  simultaneously  and  assigns  importance  to  each  position  in  the  sequence relative to the others. This is known as attention and helps to keep track of long-range dependencies in a sequence. Attention mechanisms have become an integral part of modeling complex relationships in biological sequences. Additionally, the training process on broad unlabeled data is known as pretraining and the downstream additional training on labeled data of the model to perform specific tasks is known as fine-tuning (Figure 3).

#### Nucleotide transformers and DNA foundation models

The ability of transformer-based foundation models to encode information  from  sequence  alone  has  been  applied  to  nucleotide  sequences  to  perform  prediction  and  inference  tasks  on  genomes. These models include Enformer, 57 nucleotide transformer, 58 DNABert, 59 and HyenaDNA. 60 As a case study for genomic foundation  models,  Enformer  was  used  to  effectively  predict  the  gene expression from the DNA sequence alone through the incorporation of long-range genomic interactions. Enformer used the capability of attention layers to model long-range interactions to confer interpretability to the model. On inspection of the gene attention scores, it was observed that they correlated with histone H3 acetylated at K27. The global interactions caught by the attention layer highlighted not only local  promoter  regions  but  also  distal  enhancers  more  than  20  kb away. #### Single-cell transcriptomics foundation models

In  the  previous  example,  attention  matrices  could  capture  longrange  genomic  interactions.  These  metrics  can  also  be  used  to infer  global  cell-cell  and  gene-gene  networks  from  high-dimensional  single  cell  datasets.  Geneformer  is  a  single-cell  transcriptomics foundation model trained on 30 million single-cell transcriptomics to enable context-specific predictions for different biological  networks  belonging  to  data-sparse  domains. 61 On  the prediction  side,  Geneformer  was  fine-tuned  to  perform  various tasks  such  as  prediction  of  dosage  sensitivity  for  different  genes for  better  interpretation  of  copy  number  variants  in  genetic diagnosis and predicting chromatin dynamics predictions. Geneformer is one of the many single-cell transcriptomics foundation models, which include scGPT, 62 cellFM, 63 and scBERT. 64 On the inference  side,  Geneformer  used  the  inherent  properties  of  the attention  layers  and  confirmed  that  in  the  specific  context  of aortic  endothelial  cells,  genes  paid  more  attention  to  TFs  than other  genes,  indicating  that  the  attention  layers  in  a  self-supervised  manner  learned  the  importance  of  TFs  in  dissecting  cell identity. These relationships also correlated with the most affected  genes  when  critical  TFs  were  perturbed  in  congenital heart defects. 65,66 In  a  previous  section,  we  discussed  the  importance of predicting downstream perturbation effects in developing therapeutic interventions and attention heads were also critical in the in  silico prediction of cell-state changes based on the perturbation  of  target  genes  in  cardiomyopathies,  where  the  deletion of  gene  targets  leads  the  cell  representations  to  shift  closure  to resemble  cardiomyopathic  cell  representations  from  healthy  cell representations. 61 The candidate targets were experimentally corroborated.

#### Amino acid sequence transformers and protein foundation models

Foundation models and transformers have enjoyed the greatest success in proteomics. In proteomics, AlphaFold is a widely used transformer  for  accurately  predicting  protein  structures. 67 AlphaFold predicts  the  protein  structures  as  a  graph  inference  problem  in three-dimensional  space,  where  initial  versions  utilized  multiple sequence  alignments  of  the  protein  and  succeeding  versions  used the  pairformer  architecture  directly  on  sequences.  The  success  of AlphaFold can be attributed to the biological considerations in the algorithm design process such as the encouragement to satisfy the peptide bond geometry during fine-tuning by a violation loss term and  capturing  the  evolutionary  constraint  on  structures  by  using the multiple sequence alignment of the protein as an input. #### Caveats in transformers, attention, and foundation models

Although transformers and foundation models can provide accurate predictions along with reliable inference, there are many caveats that need to be explored before their reliable and widespread application. First,  these  are  highly  parameterized  complex  models  that  require massive  amounts  of  data  for  their  pre-training.  An  undertrained transformer will generalize poorly, seriously curtailing its fine-tuning capabilities across various tasks. It is important to ascertain whether simpler  models  with  built-in  interpretability  trained  on  the  same data  can  give  comparable  performance.  This  has  been  tested  in  a few scenarios. Boiarsky et al. 73 evaluated scBERT and found that a simple logistic regression baseline outperforms or performs comparably  to  scBERT.  This  finding  held  even  in  the  few-shot  learning setting, where the model had access to a limited number of training examples. Despite the massive amounts of pre-training data, scBERT did not show any significant advantage over logistic regression. Plus, the same study reported that learning of meaningful gene representations was not necessary to perform well in the prediction objective. 73 Similarly,  Enformer  could  impute  gene  expression  from DNA sequence alone and characterize important genomic regions through its attention layers. SCARLink 52 is a simple gene-level linear model also capable of imputing gene expression from chromatin potential  without  peak  inference,  albeit  this  imputation  is  not  done solely  from  DNA sequence. However, in an interpretable manner, SCARLink  also  showed  that  predicted  genomic  regions  could capture chromosome conformation data and were significantly enriched for disease-associated genome-wide  significant variants. Although this can be a one-off instance, it underlines the need to better  evaluate  transformer  models  across  standardized  tasks  with models of different parameterizations.

A major point is the level of interpretability guaranteed by the attention layers. Attention scores and feature importance are shown to not always correlate and hence are not necessarily reliable tools for identifying  which  input  elements  are  responsible  for  an  output. 74 In certain  contexts,  rigorous  evaluation  settings  do  not  exist,  making it  difficult to ascertain whether the observed inference is critical to the model's decision-making process. In single-cell transcriptomics foundation  models,  the  attention  weights  are  used  for in  -silico perturbation  analyses;  however,  computational  benchmarking  of perturbation prediction remains challenging and has to depend on experimental  validation,  which  severely  restricts  the  confirmation of most predictions and does not produce a quantitative metric of learning evaluation. 53

### DISCUSSION

Major work in developing computational methods has been done in a targeted fashion to solve very specific and niche biological problems.  We  have  discussed  how  learning  architectures  have  been applied and evolved for better domain adaptation along with representing biological information in the most intuitive way possible. Explainability  and  interpretability  of  machine  learning  models have  found  applications  in  delineating  biological  systems  and enhanced prioritization of targets for various therapies by pinpointing sources of signal in high-dimensional albeit noisy biological datasets. We also touched upon different applications of foundation models and how a single large model can be applied to different problems across a domain. We discussed the importance of probing the amount of attention paid to different positions in the input to correlate known biological priors with the decision-making process of transformers.

#### Outlook

Despite the major advances in machine learning and biology, this progress does not happen in tandem between them. The computational biology community needs interpreters or a common language that translates the innovations between the fields for effective implementation to solve complex problems while propagating a better understanding of caveats associated with any domain. This is critical, especially since the implementation of transformers in the molecular biology space has been done in a short time since the inception of the architecture.  Although  a  meaningful  increase  in  parameters  and incorporation of  attention  scoring  has  made  it  a  successful  model across  fields,  biology  is  an  equally  convoluted  science  with  a  high level  of  unknown  parameters.  We  have  to  carefully  evaluate  new and complex models, as the molecular domains in which they will be applied are equally or more complex. As models and our knowledge  evolve,  so  will  the  challenges  in  applying  models  in  biology. Consistent algorithmic innovation driven by a deep understanding of the molecular dynamics that drive health and disease will provide pervasive optimal solutions.
