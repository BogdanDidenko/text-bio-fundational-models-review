# Single-cell exploration in natural language

Learning a programming language such as R or Python to run software is almost compulsory for a biologist who wants to explore now-omnipresent high-throughput omics data, such as those generated by single-cell RNA sequencing. With breakthroughs in artificial intelligence (AI), however, an alternative paradigm now becomes possible. "Can we turn it around and teach the computer to talk to our PhD students in the English language about their data, rather than forcing them to adapt to whatever is easy for the computer?," asks Christoph Bock, at the CeMM Research Center for Molecular Medicine and Medical University of Vienna. Motivated by this idea, he and colleagues developed CellWhisperer, a chat-based tool for exploring single-cell RNA-sequencing data.

At the heart of CellWhisperer is a multimodal embedding that jointly models single-cell transcriptome profiles and their textual annotations. To learn this embedding, the team used both gigantic gene-expression corpora from the Gene Expression Omnibus and CELLxGENE Census, as well as large language model (LLM)-assisted curation to generate compact and standardized annotations. "This is where we spent most of the GPU [graphics processing unit] hours that went into the project. Importantly, CellWhisperer is 100% open science - we provide not only the code and the model weights, but also all training data on the CellWhisperer website, for others to re-use and build upon," notes Bock.
